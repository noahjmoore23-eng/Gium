How I sell

Most sales sites list adjectives. This is the actual sequence I run, so a sales leader can tell in one read whether it matches their motion.

01

Research the operation

Before the first call I know how the prospect makes money, where the margin leaks, and which metric their boss asks about on Monday. Discovery should confirm a hypothesis, not start from zero.

02

Qualify honestly

Pain, budget, decision process, and a compelling reason to act this quarter. If one of the four is missing I say so out loud and either fix it or move on — a clean no beats a slow maybe.

03

Sell the number, not the feature

Every demo maps back to a figure the buyer already tracks. I’d rather show one screen that changes their cost per job than ten that are merely impressive.

04

Close on a plan

Mutual action plan, named signer, dated steps, and the objection surfaced early instead of on the last call. Forecast accuracy is a skill, and I treat it like one.

Case study

Gium — I built the software for the industry I sell into

Moving companies lose money in two predictable places: quotes that miss and crews that idle. An estimator eyeballs a three-bedroom house and guesses low, so two movers get sent on a job that needed four, the crew runs into overtime, and the hourly minimum never covers it. Meanwhile the dispatcher works off a whiteboard and double-books a truck.

Gium is my answer to both. It’s a working web app: a room-by-room cube sheet with 82 standard carrier items that converts an inventory into packed volume, shipment weight, recommended crew size, the smallest truck that fits, and a phase-by-phase hour breakdown. It prices off a single rate card — hourly against a minimum under 100 miles, weight-based line haul plus mileage and fuel past that, the way an interstate move is actually tariffed. Quoted jobs land on a week board that flags a mover on two overlapping jobs, a double-booked truck, or a job understaffed against its own estimate.

Why it belongs on a sales résumé: it’s proof I do the homework. I can sit across from an operations manager and talk cube sheets, access penalties, tariffs, and utilization without reaching for a script — because I modeled all of it. That’s the same preparation I bring to a territory.
